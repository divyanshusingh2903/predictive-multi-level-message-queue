\subsection{Production flow and its replication}
Predictive routing passed the local gate (Table~\ref{tab:prodflow}): mean latency \VTwoMeanVsFifo{} and short-class
median \VTwoShortPFiftyVsFifo{} against FIFO, with all-message P99 at \VTwoPNinetyNine{}. It beat the hand-tuned static
map (\VTwoVsTuned{}) and the misconfigured one (\VTwoVsMisconf{}), and with no job labels it still gained
\VTwoZeroConfig{}. The AWS replication on a separate host reproduced every verdict (mean \EOneMeanVsFifo{}, short-class
median \EOneShortPFiftyVsFifo{}). The predictor is statistically close to the oracle reference.

\subsection{Against RabbitMQ and Redis}
On the same workload (Table~\ref{tab:brokers}), \sys{}'s predictive arm cut mean latency \EThreeVsRabbit{} against
RabbitMQ FIFO and \EThreeVsRedis{} against Redis FIFO. \sys{}'s own FIFO is slower than RabbitMQ's by
\EThreeFifoGap{}, outside the pre-registered equivalence bar, which narrows the gain. The predictor transfers: running
as a sidecar on RabbitMQ it cut mean latency \EThreeSidecar{}, more than the in-broker arm, plausibly because RabbitMQ
priorities never age; this is an explanation, not a measured cause. Static priorities without aging starved the long
class (maximum wait \EThreeStaticLongWait{} FIFO's). With weights and pausing aging, \sys{} reached \EThreeWeighted{}.
In a broker-cost microbenchmark \sys{} had the lowest peak throughput of the three (about 5,300 deliveries/s versus
7,400 for RabbitMQ and 21,000 for Redis).

\subsection{Traces}
Table~\ref{tab:traces} shows the pattern. With default aging (gate~A) the predictor passes only once; on hours-long
backlogs even the oracle is within 1--3\% of FIFO. With weights and pausing aging (gate~B), the gate passes on five of
six Azure and Huawei cells. On the two HPC logs no gate passes: on LPC-EGEE most keys are stale or cold when a job
arrives, so most jobs take the default level; on DAS2-fs2 gate~B helps short jobs several-fold but long jobs, which
carry most of the work, wait longer, and the P99 and long-job guardrails fail. The oracle with the same scheduler
improves every window there, so the loss is in prediction, not in the scheduler. On Azure, a per-key median predicts
held-out durations to within a factor of \AzurePerKeyErrFactor{} (median) against \AzureGlobalErrFactor{} for a
single global median (Table~\ref{tab:predictability}).
