## Why this experiment

Two weaknesses in the paper are addressed. First, the predictor and aging parameters were fixed by judgement and never
varied, and the Azure trace is in-sample for the weighted design, so we do not know whether the defaults sit on a cliff.
Second, the trace intervals treat seven adjacent windows as independent, but backlogs carry across windows, so the
intervals are probably too narrow.

## Part A: one-at-a-time sensitivity

**Question.** Does the verdict of the paper's trace gates change when one parameter at a time is moved off its default?

**Design.** Each variant changes one parameter and keeps all others at their defaults. The default is in bold. Each
variant runs both arms of interest, `predictive_warm` (gate A) and `predictive_warm+weights+pausing` (gate B), and
`fifo` for reference, at **offered load 0.8 only** (the load at which the paper's gate B result is clearest and the
oracle gap is largest), on both traces.

| Parameter | Values | Config field |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum observations `n_min` | 5, **20**, 100 | `predictor.min_samples` |
| Spread ratio κ (99th percentile) | 4, **16**, 64, ∞ | `predictor.max_spread_ratio` |
| Key decay `d` | 0.98, **0.995**, 0.999, 1.0 | `predictor.decay` |
| Staleness `τ` | 5 min, **30 min**, 2 h, off | `stale_after_ms` |
| Aging threshold | 1 s, **5 s**, 30 s | `aging.threshold_ms` |
| Level weights | **(8, 3, 1)**, (4, 2, 1), (16, 4, 1) | `level_weights` |
| Number of levels | 2, **3**, 4 | `levels` |

That is 18 distinct variants (17 off-default values and the default), on two traces and two arms.

**Primary measure.** For each variant, the paired relative change in mean latency against FIFO (by window), as in
`v3-traces-1`.

**Criteria (descriptive, no gate for the paper's claim):**

| ID | Reading | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | **robust to a parameter** | the gate's P1 criterion (≤ −15%, interval below 0) gives the same verdict at every value of that parameter as at its default |
| R2 | **sensitive to a parameter** | the verdict flips at one or more values; the paper names the parameter and the range |
| R3 | **default on a cliff** | the verdict flips at a value one grid step from the default |

The paper reports, for each trace and arm, the share of variants that pass P1 and the full table. If R2 or R3 holds for
any parameter, the paper says the result is sensitive to it; it does not move the defaults and re-report.

**What is not claimed.** One-at-a-time sweeps miss interactions. Load 0.8 only, on two traces. This tests robustness of
the defaults, not that they are optimal.

## Part B: block-bootstrap re-estimation of the trace intervals

**Question.** Do the pre-registered trace verdicts survive intervals that respect autocorrelation between adjacent
windows?

**Design.** Re-run the `v3-traces-1` arms (all eight, both traces, loads 0.5, 0.8, 0.95) with the evaluation span cut
into **28 equal-duration windows** instead of seven (`evaluation_days: 28`; the field counts windows). Everything else
is unchanged. The aggregate (`all`) metrics are independent of the window count, so the simulator must reproduce
`v3-traces-1`'s aggregate numbers exactly; this is the first check, and any difference stops the experiment.

**Analysis.** The paired relative change per window is resampled with a **moving-block bootstrap**: 10,000 resamples,
seed 2026, **block length 4** (four consecutive windows, equal to one seventh of the span, the same as the old
window). Intervals for block lengths 1 (independent windows) and 2 are reported alongside, so the effect of
dependence is visible. Block length 4 is primary. It was chosen before any run, to match the old seven-window length and the hours-long
backlogs of the Azure trace, not from the data.

**Criteria.**

| ID | Rule |
|---|---|
| T1 | each of the 12 gate cells (2 gates × 2 traces × 3 loads) is re-evaluated with its pre-registered thresholds and the block-length-4 interval |
| T2 | the paper reports every cell whose verdict differs from `v3-traces-1`, in either direction |

**Either way:** the block-bootstrap intervals replace the independent-window intervals in the paper's trace figure
and table, with the old ones kept in the appendix. If fewer cells pass than before, the paper says so.

### Choices made while writing the generator (not in the table above; confirm before freezing)

- The aging **interval** stays one tenth of the threshold: 1000/100 ms and 30000/3000 ms, against 5000/500.
- Level weights for the **number of levels** variants: (8, 1) for two levels and (8, 4, 2, 1) for four. The default tier
  stays 1. The table gave no weights for these.
- The metric classes (`class_bounds_ms` 100 and 1000 ms) stay fixed whatever the number of levels, so variants are
  compared on the same classes. The `predictive` arms' tier boundaries are the predictor's own and do change.
- Only the six gate criteria (A and B: P1, G1, G2) are evaluated per variant; the R readings use P1.
- Part A uses the seven windows of `v3-traces-1`, so its intervals are the independent-window ones; Part B is where
  dependence is handled.

## What has been seen before freezing

The `v3-traces-1` results on both traces are published and were seen. **No variant of Part A has been simulated** and
**no 28-window run exists**. The grid and the block length were written before either was run. The Azure results are
in-sample for the weighted design (see the paper's table of what informed each experiment); Part A therefore tests
robustness around defaults chosen with Azure in view, and the paper says so.

## Threats

- 18 variants × 2 traces × 2 arms is a lot of cells; the paper reports all of them and does not pick the interesting
  ones. No multiplicity correction is applied because the experiment makes no pass/fail claim.
- With 28 windows, Azure's are 6 hours long and Huawei's, whose evaluation half is 12 hours, about 26 minutes, so short windows cut across backlogs. Block bootstrap reduces but does not remove the dependence.
- Huawei is one region and one day; Part A there says nothing about other days.
